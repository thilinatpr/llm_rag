## 104 | FR 104 - Investigation of losses, reporting of losses/damage and determining those responsible (replaced by PF Circular 02/2024, from 01.06.2024)
PF Circular 02/2024 (29.05.2024) replaces FR 104(1) and (2) with effect from 01.06.2024. Background given in the circular: Accounting Officers must make adequate provision so that surcharges are imposed, under the procedure in the Financial Regulations, on officers/employees whose negligence, carelessness, wrongdoing or fraud caused a loss to the Government, and the Chief Accounting Officer must ensure they do so; the Committee on Public Accounts has drawn attention to losses, destruction and irregular payments in State institutions that were not properly dealt with.

FR 104(1)(a) As soon as a damage or loss occurs, an investigation shall be started to find its extent and cause precisely and to determine those responsible.

FR 104(1)(b) For a damage or loss valued at Rs. 2,000,000 or more, a preliminary investigation shall be started immediately, until the Chief Accounting Officer appoints a board of inquiry. The chairman of the board of inquiry shall be a senior-level officer as defined in Public Administration Circular 03/2016. For a damage or loss valued below Rs. 2,000,000, the Accounting Officer shall appoint a board of inquiry; if the value is between Rs. 1,000,000 and Rs. 2,000,000, a copy of the letter of appointment shall be sent to the Chief Accounting Officer for information.

FR 104(2) If the value of the loss/damage (as in FR 105(1)) exceeds Rs. 750,000, a copy of the report shall be sent to the General Treasury (Department of Public Finance). Unless fraud, negligence or another offence has occurred or is involved, the following losses/damage need not be reported to the General Treasury:
(a) losses of stores etc. not exceeding Rs. 25,000 that do not involve a write-off from the books;
(b) losses not exceeding Rs. 25,000 that are fully recovered (including losses from over-payments or wrong payments on paysheets, travel claims etc., but not the shortages in (c));
if any such loss cannot be recovered fully or promptly, the amount shall be recovered as a surcharge from the officers responsible for the loss and/or for not recovering it;
(c) shortages of cash, stamps, licences etc. within the limits below, if the officer(s) responsible make them good immediately: (i) in institutions where cash, licences, stamps etc. are checked daily: a shortage not exceeding Rs. 25,000 in value; (ii) where there is no daily check: a shortage not exceeding Rs. 15,000 in value.
These exceptions concern the financial side only; they do not mean that disciplinary action need not be taken where it is required.

General instructions (02/2024 para 04): the Chief Accounting Officer/Accounting Officer must make sure the investigation under FR 104 and the preliminary and full reports under FR 104(3) and 104(4) are completed within the time limits in the Financial Regulations; if the investigation is or has been delayed for an unavoidable reason (e.g. the value of a loss cannot be assessed because it is the subject of a court case), the Accounting Officer/board of inquiry shall inform the Chief Accounting Officer and obtain proper approval. Acting under the circular is no bar to investigations, or to legal or administrative action, on write-offs. Amounts shown in the accounts as losses/damage/arrears of revenue because of accounting errors shall be removed by correcting the accounts. All pending losses and damage shall be dealt with before 31.12.2024; after that, Chief Accounting Officers shall make sure that requests to the General Treasury to write off losses and damage more than two years old are not sent, except those that can be considered under 04(i). For State corporations, statutory boards, State banks, universities and Government-owned companies, losses are dealt with under the Department of Public Enterprises' Guidelines on Good Governance, operations manual and related circulars.

## 108 | FR 108 - Authorities to deal with losses (amended by PF Circular 01/2020 from 01.09.2020 and by PF Circular 02/2024 from 01.06.2024)
Current text (PF Circular 02/2024, 29.05.2024, in force 01.06.2024; it amends FR 108 as already amended by Part I para 2 of PF Circular 01/2020):

FR 108(1) Unless a new policy is involved, the officers below may deal with losses up to these limits (each loss). Current limits from 01.06.2024; the limit before 01.06.2024 (PF Circular 01/2020) is in brackets.
(a) Chief Accounting Officer who is a Secretary to a Ministry:
- normal wastage (normal evaporation and leakage, vaporisation, deterioration etc.): within the percentages fixed by the Treasury (to be published later);
- losses at sea or in the air: Rs. 7,000,000 (before: Rs. 5,000,000);
- losses from waiving contract terms with traders, shipping agents or cargo-handling companies etc.: Rs. 7,000,000 (before: Rs. 5,000,000);
- other losses as in FR 105(1): Rs. 3,000,000 (before: Rs. 2,000,000);
- other losses where there was fraud, a defect in the existing system or lack of supervision: Rs. 7,000,000 (before: Rs. 5,000,000).
(b) Chief Accounting Officer who is not a Secretary to a Ministry: the same limits as in (a) (before: Rs. 1,000,000 for losses as in FR 105(1)).
(c) Accounting Officer:
- losses as in FR 105(1): Rs. 500,000 (before: Rs. 250,000);
- losses that need not be reported to the Treasury under FR 104(2): the limit in FR 104(2) is Rs. 750,000 (before: Rs. 50,000);
- losses of stores etc. not involving a write-off from the books, FR 104(2)(a): Rs. 25,000 (before: Rs. 500);
- losses fully recovered, FR 104(2)(b): Rs. 25,000 (before: Rs. 500);
- shortages of cash, stamps, licences etc. made good immediately, FR 104(2)(c): Rs. 25,000 where there is a daily check (before: Rs. 250), Rs. 15,000 where there is no daily check (before: Rs. 25).

FR 108(2) The authority to deal with losses under FR 108(1) includes: (i) recovering the lost money from officers as a surcharge; (ii) authorising recovery from securities, insurance etc.; (iii) giving the final order to remove the amount from the books under FR 109(1); and (iv) approving a write-off under FR 109.

FR 108(3) Only where a Chief Accounting Officer has no authority within the limits in FR 108(1) shall full details, with the recommendation of the Chief Accounting Officer/Accounting Officer, be reported to the General Treasury (Department of Public Finance) for suitable orders; the Department of Public Finance will obtain suitable orders from the Secretary to the Treasury or a Deputy Secretary to the Treasury. Requests sent without acting within the limits in which the Chief Accounting Officer has authority will not be considered by the General Treasury.

FR 108(4) General Forms 283, 284 and 285 shall be used to report losses under FR 104(3) and 104(4) and/or to obtain approval to write off from the books of accounts under FR 109; they shall be duly completed and submitted, as appropriate, to the General Treasury (Department of Public Finance) and/or to the Chief Accounting Officer or Accounting Officer authorised under FR 108(1).

FR 108(5) The Secretary to the Treasury has power to revise the value limits in the table under FR 108(1).

Statutory boards and similar institutions (PF Circular 01/2020 para 2.2, not changed by 02/2024): State institutions that, as stated in the "Preface" to the Financial Regulations 1992, have not had their own detailed financial regulations and procedures duly approved, and statutory boards and similar institutions that must follow the Financial Regulations for financial control (para 06 of the "Foreword"), deal with losses applying "Losses and Write-off" in Part 3 of Chapter II: (2.2.1) where a loss under FR 105(1) does not exceed Rs. 500,000, the institution's Board of Directors has power to deal with it; (2.2.2) where it exceeds Rs. 500,000, the Secretary to the Ministry supervising the institution, as Chief Accounting Officer, has power to deal with it within the limits in the table; (2.2.3) where the Chief Accounting Officer has no authority under 2.2.1 and 2.2.2, full details are reported to the General Treasury (to the Director General of Public Enterprises or the Director General of National Budget, as relevant), which forwards them with observations and recommendations to the Director General of Public Finance.

## 113 | FR 113(3) - Other write-offs: arrears of revenue and fees (amended by PF Circular 01/2024 from 01.06.2024)
Background (PF Circular 01/2024, 29.05.2024): Accounting Officers and revenue Accounting Officers must make special provision to collect arrears of revenue and other amounts due to the Government (including loan balances of the advance "B" account) within the prescribed time, and the Chief Accounting Officer must ensure they do so (as stated in the letter appointing a Chief Accounting Officer under FR 124). They should take quick decisions within their delegated powers and seek General Treasury approval only where a matter is beyond their authority. The Committee on Public Accounts has drawn attention to arrears of revenue and of advance "B" loans that were not properly recovered.

FR 113(3) - Other write-offs (01/2024 para 02, from 01.06.2024):
(i) Revenue estimates shall be prepared as realistically as possible and errors found during the year corrected within that year. Amounts shown as arrears of revenue because of accounting errors shall be removed by correcting the accounts.
(ii) Only when the Chief Accounting Officer is satisfied, after every action has been taken to recover irrecoverable Government revenue or other fees due under FR 113(3)(a) and (b), that keeping the balances any longer is futile, may the Chief Accounting Officer write them off, up to a maximum of Rs. 100,000 in each separate case. (This limit is for revenue and fees under FR 113(3), not for advance "B" loans: those are under FR 113(6) below.)
(iii) A request to the Secretary to the Treasury for a write-off under FR 113(3)(c) is needed only where the Chief Accounting Officer has no authority under (ii), i.e. above the Rs. 100,000 limit.

## 113 | FR 113(6) - Write-off of loans and advances under the advance "B" account of Government officers (added by PF Circular 01/2020; replaced by 01/2024 from 01.06.2024; interim limits extended by 01/2024 (I))
FR 113(6) - Write-off of loans and advances given under the advance "B" account (inserted by PF Circular 01/2020 para 3; replaced by 01/2024 para 03):
(a) When a loan or advance is written off under this regulation, its value may be removed from the books only after proper authority to write it off has been given in writing.
(b) The Government does not expect any loss from loans or advances given under the advance "B" account, so every possible step shall be taken to recover them. How to recover them is set out in Guideline No. 01 to FR 113(6) (issued with PF Circular 01/2020), available on the Department of Public Finance website.
(c) After the Chief Accounting Officer is personally satisfied that every administrative action in Guideline No. 01 has been taken, act under the rules and limits of the interim provisions below. They apply where loans/advances still cannot be recovered when officers who were not required to give valid sureties vacate post or leave the service, when the appointing authority ends an officer's service, on dismissal or interdiction, or on the officer's death.
Interim provisions (01/2024 para 04, as replaced by 01/2024 (I), 24.01.2025): to write off long-outstanding loan balances that cannot now be removed from the accounts and books, Chief Accounting Officers are authorised within these special limits, where the default or recovery has stopped: loan balance outstanding (i) less than 5 years: not exceeding Rs. 25,000 per officer; (ii) 5-10 years: not exceeding Rs. 50,000 per officer; (iii) 10-20 years: not exceeding Rs. 75,000 per officer; (iv) more than 20 years: not exceeding Rs. 100,000 per officer. Chief Accounting Officers/Accounting Officers shall attend to this personally and give written orders to the Chief Financial Officer/Chief Accountant/Additional Secretaries/Senior Assistant Secretaries or other officers. Balances that cannot be dealt with within these limits shall be reported, with reasons and the possible next steps, to the Director General of Public Finance before 31.03.2025 (01/2024 had said 30.09.2024). The delegated authority is valid only until 31.03.2025 (01/2024 had said 31.12.2024), and no balance of the advance "B" account more than 5 years old shall be carried beyond 31.03.2025, except balances under legal action or court cases at that date; problems are to be resolved on the advice of the Department of Public Finance. Details of the balances written off for each officer shall be reported to the Department of Public Finance before 15.04.2025.
Summary of the write-off limits for advance "B" loan balances: from 01.06.2024 to 31.03.2025, the interim limits above by age of the balance (Rs. 25,000 / 50,000 / 75,000 / 100,000 per officer). Before 01.06.2024, PF Circular 01/2020 allowed the Chief Accounting Officer to write off up to Rs. 25,000 per case (officers not entitled to a pension who gave no valid sureties and left the service). The original para 05 of 01/2024 said that this Rs. 25,000 limit applies again when the interim provisions end; 01/2024 (I) replaced para 05 with the reporting date (15.04.2025) and does not say which limit applies after 31.03.2025, so check with the Department of Public Finance. Other provisions of 01/2024 continue (01/2024 (I) para 03).
General (01/2024 para 06-07): a request to the General Treasury to write off loan balances must state, with the Chief Accounting Officer's personal recommendation, that Guideline No. 01 was followed. Acting under the circular is no bar to investigations or to legal or administrative action. State corporations, statutory boards, State banks, universities and Government-owned companies follow the Department of Public Enterprises' Guidelines on Good Governance and operations manual.

## 124 | FR 124(2) - Supervision by a Secretary to a Cabinet Ministry or a State Ministry as Chief Accounting Officer (replaced by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
FR 124(2)(a) The Minister of Finance is responsible to Parliament for all receipts and payments and must name officers to collect and spend public money on his behalf. The Minister of Finance therefore appoints the Secretary of each Cabinet Ministry as the Chief Accounting Officer of that Ministry and, subject to Treasury directions, delegates to him the responsibility (as Chief Accounting Officer) for supervising the financial work of departments. Accordingly the Secretary to the Cabinet Ministry is the Chief Accounting Officer for the State Ministry and for the departments, State corporations and statutory institutions under the Cabinet Ministry.
FR 124(2)(b) The Minister of Finance appoints the Secretary of each State Ministry as the Chief Accounting Officer of that State Ministry, with the same delegated responsibility, and the State Ministry Secretary is the Chief Accounting Officer for the departments, State corporations and statutory institutions under the State Ministry.
FR 124(2)(c) Besides Secretaries to Cabinet Ministries and State Ministries, the officers in charge of other departments/State institutions specified in Article 52(4) of the Constitution and not supervised by Secretaries are Chief Accounting Officers.
While the general control of the Government's financial work rests with the Treasury, this arrangement lets the Secretary/Chief Accounting Officer fulfil the constitutional responsibility of supervising the financial transactions of those departments.

## 125 | FR 125(1) - Secretaries as Accounting Officers for their own Ministry office (amended by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
FR 125(1)(a) Every Secretary to a Cabinet Ministry, in addition to being the Chief Accounting Officer for the Cabinet Ministry and the departments, State corporations and statutory institutions under it, is also the Accounting Officer for his own Ministry office.
FR 125(1)(b) (new) Every Secretary to a State Ministry, in addition to being the Chief Accounting Officer for the State Ministry and the departments, State corporations and statutory institutions under it, is also the Accounting Officer for his own State Ministry office.

## 238 | FR 238(5) - Paysheets: salaries paid into bank accounts (replaced by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
Steps shall be taken to send the net salary of every public sector employee to a bank account opened in the officer's own name. The paying officer must confirm that every officer receiving a monthly salary has signed the paysheet. This does not prevent sending part of an employee's salary, at his request, to another bank account kept in the officer's own name, but the number of bank accounts to which money is sent is limited to two.

## 269 | FR 269 - Certificate that the officer is alive (replaced by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
(1) When (a) the salary of an officer on leave, or out of the island on duty, is paid in Sri Lanka, or (b) the salary of an officer on leave in Sri Lanka is paid at a place other than that in FR 268, the paying officer shall be given each month not only a receipt for the money but also a certificate from a responsible person confirming that the officer is alive.
(2) For an officer who goes abroad on a scholarship, a certificate from a responsible person confirming that he is alive, submitted once every 6 months, is sufficient.
The paying head of department may disregard this rule if he knows that the officer is alive.

## 371 | FR 371 - Ad-hoc sub-imprests (amended by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
FR 371(1) Subject to what follows, an Accounting Officer may issue part of his own imprest to any of his subordinate officers; such a re-issue is called an "ad-hoc sub-imprest". No officer other than an Accounting Officer may re-issue any part of an imprest given to him by the General Treasury without the published authority of the Treasury (Department of Treasury Operations).
FR 371(2) An Accounting Officer may give an ad-hoc sub-imprest only for:
(a) Petty cash: for day-to-day petty expenses. An Accounting Officer may, at his discretion, allow any officer serving under the direct supervision of a staff officer to keep a petty-cash ad-hoc sub-imprest of up to Rs. 25,000. The most that may be paid from it for one requirement at a time is Rs. 5,000.
(b) Special purpose: up to Rs. 100,000 on one occasion, only to staff-grade officers, for a specific task. The staff officer shall settle it as soon as the task is completed.
Where the limits in (a) and (b) must be exceeded, the Accounting Officer shall obtain the prior approval of the General Treasury (Department of Treasury Operations) by a request with the Chief Accounting Officer's recommendation and enough facts to confirm the need.
(c) Continuous ad-hoc sub-imprest: an advance for all payments of a sub-office; it must always be paid into the sub-office's official bank account. Proposals must be approved by the Director General of Treasury Operations and requested only through the Chief Accounting Officer.
FR 371(3) Bank pre-paid cards introduced by the General Treasury may also be used for payments from ad-hoc sub-imprests under FR 371(2).
FR 371(4) An Accounting Officer shall not obtain ad-hoc sub-imprests for unspecified or unnecessary purposes, and may further restrict their use at his discretion.
FR 371(5) The officer receiving an ad-hoc sub-imprest shall render accounts under the procedure and conditions laid down by the Accounting Officer. The unspent balance shall be repaid when the Accounting Officer so decides or earlier, and in any event on or before 31 December of the financial year in which it was issued. An ad-hoc sub-imprest shall be settled within 10 days of completing the task.
FR 371(6) The rule that money given as an "advance" for one financial year may not be used for payments of another financial year applies also to ad-hoc sub-imprests.

## 756 | FR 756(6) - Annual board of survey: time frame (added by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
Time frame for the annual board of survey of stores, inventories and stock (first date: Ministry/Department/District Secretariat with no provincial or regional offices; second date: with provincial or regional offices):
01 Appoint the boards of survey for the financial year: before 15 December of that financial year (both).
02 Complete the physical board of survey work: before 31 January of the next financial year / before 15 March.
03 Obtain the board of survey report: before 10 February / before 31 March.
04 Report and act on shortages and surpluses in the report: before 28 February / before 15 April.
05 Take suitable action on the goods in the General 47 reports: before 15 March / before 30 April.
06 Send the board of survey report to the Auditor General: before 31 March / before 15 June.
07 Inform the Director General of Public Finance that 01-06 are complete: before 10 April / before 30 June.
Only a copy of the letter sending the report to the Auditor General (item 06) need be sent to the Department of Public Finance (item 07); the reports themselves are not sent. Where several boards were appointed under the institution of an Accounting Officer, a summary confirming that all reports went to the Auditor General and that the previous year's board work is complete shall be sent to the Department of Public Finance on or before 30 July of the current year (form on the Department of Public Finance website).

## 767 | FR 767(1) - Usable surplus stores and equipment (replaced by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
Usable surplus goods identified at the annual board of survey, or when the Chief Accounting Officer or Accounting Officer acts under FR 756(4) - goods the institution does not need but another institution can use - may be handed over to a Government Ministry, Department, technical college, Government vocational training institution or Government school that needs them, by inventory transfer (FR 753), with written evidence of handing over and taking over (General 141 for issue and General 219 for receipt).

## 770 | FR 770 - Unusable goods: board of survey; disposal of unusable vehicles (FR 770(3) replaced and FR 770(4) added by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
FR 770(3) Where the board recommends destroying an item whose purchase cost is not less than Rs. 50,000, the Chief Accounting Officer/Accounting Officer shall approve its destruction only after obtaining a report from an independent Government officer with technical expertise on that item.
FR 770(4) Disposal of unusable vehicles:
(a) A special board of survey shall be appointed to identify unusable vehicles lying idle in Government institutions and classify them as (i) wrecks, (ii) not in running condition and not economical to repair, and (iii) in running condition but not economical to use, and/or identified for disposal on other special grounds.
(b) A valuation committee of at most 5 members shall value them, chaired by a Class 1 officer of an island-wide service where the institution has an expenditure head in the national budget, or by a senior staff officer for a State enterprise. Vehicles identified for disposal shall be disposed of by calling open bids, with the approval of the Chief Accounting Officer/Accounting Officer, or, for State enterprises, of the Chief Accounting Officer on the Board of Directors' recommendation. Only where disposal by open bids is difficult may the Chief Accounting Officer decide to sell by public auction, so that the Government suffers no financial loss.
(c) Auctioneers: only where one institution disposes of more than 50 vehicles at one time, and the Chief Accounting Officer personally confirms that registered auctioneers are needed, may a suitable auctioneer be selected under the Government procurement guidelines.
(d) Vehicles shall be handed over to successful bidders, and released from the auction premises, only after the full amount has been recovered; all revenue shall be credited to the Consolidated Fund under the relevant revenue head.
(e) Detailed guidance on removing idle vehicles of State institutions is on the Department of Public Finance website.

## 855 | FR 855 - Purchase and disposal of newspapers (Chapter XIV Part 5 replaced by PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
FR 855(1)(a) Local newspapers may be bought at Government expense with the Accounting Officer's approval. Since State institutions now work through the internet and all printed newspapers can be read electronically, the Accounting Officer shall limit buying local newspapers from Government funds as far as possible and tell officers to read newspapers on the internet.
(b) Departments shall not subscribe to foreign newspapers at Government expense without the approval of the Secretary to the relevant Ministry.
FR 855(2) If newspapers are bought for an officer from Government funds, a nominal 15% of their value shall be recovered from the officer. An officer who wishes to take newspapers bought in common for a Ministry, Provincial Council, Department, District Secretariat or a sub-office, after official and daily use, may be given them after recovering a nominal 10% of their value.

## 876 | FR 876 - Management of statutory funds, trust funds and departmental funds (new, PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
(1) New funds: under Article 149 of the Constitution, to maintain public financial discipline, collecting revenue and spending outside the budget approved by Appropriation Acts is not approved; creating new funds other than by an Act approved by Parliament in a very special case is completely prohibited.
(2) Statutory and trust funds now operating: (a) if running an existing fund is a national need and the fund has not been approved by Parliament through a suitable Act, Parliament's approval shall be obtained within one year of the issue of this circular; (b) if there is no need to continue, or its objectives can be met within the budget approved by Appropriation Acts, steps shall be taken at once to wind the fund up; (c) after settling the payments and other liabilities of a fund being wound up, the remaining money shall be transferred to the Consolidated Fund; investments in outside institutions shall also be transferred to the Consolidated Fund as soon as they mature.
(3) Non-statutory funds shall be closed with immediate effect and their balances credited to the Consolidated Fund; if there are reasonable grounds to continue one, steps shall be taken to legalise it by an Act of Parliament.

## 877 | FR 877 - Budget, accounts, financial statements and annual performance reports of statutory and trust funds (new, PF Circular 01/2020, from 01.09.2020)
(1) Budget: (a) a fund's financial year runs from 1 January to 31 December; (b) all inquiries about foreign aid for a fund shall be made only through the Director General of External Resources; (c) the fund's administrator shall each year prepare the next year's budget in line with the fund's objective, obtain the approval of the Secretary to the Ministry and the Minister, and send it to the Director General of National Budget for Treasury approval before 30 September of the current year, with a copy to the Director General of Public Finance; (d) no expenditure may be incurred under a fund in a financial year without a budget approved for that year.
(2) Accounts, financial statements and performance reports: (a) each fund shall have its own bank account; (b) no expenditure of the fund shall be incurred without authority under the fund's statutory provisions or under the Financial Regulations; (c) guidance and forms for accounting, quarterly reports and final accounts are on the Department of Public Finance website; (d) within 2 months after the end of the accounting year, certified financial statements with a performance report shall be sent to the Auditor General, with a copy to the Department of Public Finance; (e) every Accounting Officer shall prepare the annual performance report in all three languages under the prescribed provisions, including the Auditor General's audit reports on the fund, and submit it to Parliament for tabling before 150 days after the end of the financial year, following the instructions of the Secretary to the Office of the Leader of the House and Parliamentary rules and practice.
